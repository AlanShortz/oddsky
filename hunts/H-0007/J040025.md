# J040025.77+323527.4: a candidate microlensing event. A red giant was 2.7 times brighter for a day in August 2017

## Finding

In the NEOWISE visit of 2017 Aug 27.56 - Aug 28.54 UT (MJD 57992.56-57993.54) the star was brighter by a factor of
**2.731 in W1 (3.4 µm) and 2.716 in W2 (4.6 µm)**. In each of the other 20 NEOWISE visits (2014 Feb - 2024 Feb) it is within
0.03 mag of its median. A day-long, factor-of-three brightening that is identical at 3.4 and 4.6 µm, on a red-clump giant with no sign of
activity, binarity or accretion, is what a gravitational microlensing event looks like. The source sits toward the
Galactic anticentre, where events are rare.

## Evidence (every number reproducible from the files named)

- **Observed:** 13 single exposures in the event visit, W1 = 10.035 (rms 0.015), W2 = 10.134 (rms 0.020); baseline from
  246 exposures in other visits, W1 = 11.126, W2 = 11.218 (`work/exp_J040025.parquet`, IRSA `neowiser_p1bs_psd`, 90" cone).
- **Computed:** magnification A(W1) = 2.731, A(W2) = 2.716; W1−W2 = −0.099 ± 0.008 during the event against −0.091 baseline
  (change −0.008 ± 0.008). For a point-source, point-lens event, A = 2.73 means the source was u = 0.386 Einstein radii from the
  lens at the time of the visit.
- **Computed:** across the visit W1 brightens by 0.037 ± 0.011 mag/day (W2 +0.002 ± 0.020): a 2.8σ rise. The referee's point-lens grid
  fit, which includes the flat visits 6 months before and after, gives tE = 6-53 d (95%) and a peak between 1 day before and 12 days
  after the visit (`reviews/claude-referee-2026-10-29/a5.py`). Our earlier "tE ≳ 20 d" from the slope was wrong: near the peak the slope
  locates the peak and says little about tE.
- **Observed:** centroid offset from the star during the event 0.08" (median), the same as other visits; one detection per
  frame within 2.5"; reduced χ² 1.2-8.9 (median 2.6; other visits have visit medians of 1.1-4.0).
- **Observed:** 211 stars with W1 < 12.5 within 20' in the same frames (referee, a3/a4.py). The 30 at the target's brightness (W1 10-11)
  changed by +0.002 ± 0.007 mag; the target is the only star that moved by more than 0.3 mag.
- **Observed:** ZTF DR (2018 Mar - 2025, catflags = 0): r = 13.95, rms 0.011. Nothing comparable. ZTF began after the
  event.

## Novelty assessment (searched 2026-10-29)

Not found in: VSX (VizieR mirror B/vsx/vsx, 6"), TNS (30"), the Gaia Science Alerts final list (27,076 alerts), Gaia DR3
`vari_microlensing` (its window ends 2017 May 28, before the event), Gaia DR3 `vari_classifier_result`. A web search found nothing for
the Gaia id or the designation (referee). SIMBAD and ADS were not reachable (CDS down). Gaia observed the star on 2017 Jul 27, Aug 22
and Sep 18 (GOST, `reviews/claude-referee-2026-10-29/gost_2017jun-oct.csv`). It raised no alert, but the predicted ΔG ≈ −0.8 on Aug 22
is below the ~1 mag threshold the alert pipeline used in 2017. OGLE, KMTNet and MOA do not observe this field. **Apparently unreported**, but not independently
confirmed as novel.

## Alternative explanations

| explanation | status |
|---|---|
| NEOWISE artifact (ghost, latent, scattered light, bad frame calibration) | disfavoured: 13 exposures across 11 scans over 1 day agree; 18 control stars flat; centroid fixed |
| AllWISE association flip / deblending (killed two fades in this hunt) | disfavoured: one detection per frame, rchi2 normal |
| Moving object crossing the star | disfavoured: it would have to stay within 0.1" for 24 h and have stellar W1−W2 |
| Flare, or an outburst of an unseen hot companion (CV, symbiotic) | disfavoured: a hot added source supplying 63% of the light would redden W1−W2 by ≈ +0.06; observed −0.008 ± 0.008. A flare with this energy (≈ 56 L☉ in the infrared alone, for ≥ 1 day) is not physical |
| Unrelated transient within 0.1" | not excluded, but it would need stellar colours |
| **Microlensing by a foreground star or remnant** | consistent with everything measured |

## Skeptical review

Referee report: [`reviews/claude-referee-2026-10-29.md`](reviews/claude-referee-2026-10-29.md). It survives every instrumental,
association and physics attack. **P(mundane) = 0.15**, driven by the prior (the optical depth on this line of sight is ~1.1×10⁻⁸; about
0.07 such events were expected among the hunt's anticentre stars) and by the lack of a second instrument.

## Optical confirmation: ATLAS (2026-10-29)

ATLAS forced photometry (task 5270459; c/o epochs 2015 Oct - 2026 Oct; `work/atlas_J040025.txt`; figure
[`figures/J040025_atlas.png`](figures/J040025_atlas.png)) shows **one smooth rise and fall in 2017 and nothing else in eleven years.**

- **Observed** (o-band flux / baseline, nightly): ×1.51 (Jul 30), ×1.61 (Aug 5), ×2.43 (Aug 24), **×2.79 (Aug 28, the night NEOWISE
  measured ×2.73 in W1 and ×2.72 in W2)**, ×3.30 (Sep 1), ×5.9 (Sep 11), **×7.3 (Sep 13, the brightest night ATLAS caught; m = 11.6, near
  ATLAS saturation)**, ×3.7 (Sep 27), ×2.8 (Oct 1), ×2.6 (Oct 3), ×1.30 (Oct 29), ×1.11 (Nov 16), ×1.04 (Nov 22, c).
- **No repeat:** no other night in 2015-2026 is more than 15% above baseline in c. The single exception in o (2022 Jan 1, ×1.46) is one
  frame from the shallow 03a unit (limit 17.4); the frames before and after are normal.
- **Gaia:** it observed the star on 2017 Jul 27, Aug 22, and on Sep 18 (MJD 58014.7, 3.5 days after the peak) in a **single field of view**
  only. *Inferred, unverified:* Gaia Science Alerts needs both fields of view, which would explain why it never alerted.

Status of the event: **reproduced** (two independent instruments, NEOWISE and ATLAS, same event on the same night).

## The light-curve fit (`scripts/hunt_h0007_lensfit.py`; second referee: `reviews/claude-referee-2-2026-10-29.md`)

Inputs: ATLAS binned per night and band, error floor from the baseline night-to-night scatter plus 1% of flux, points with m < 12.2 excluded
(near saturation), one dropout night (MJD 58185.3, ×0.62) dropped, and the NEOWISE visit included.

- **Computed:** the light curve is single and smooth but **asymmetric**. A symmetric point-lens curve is rejected (χ² 462/69 against 98/67 for
  annual parallax; Δχ² 85-490 across every systematics variant the referee tried, and in each ATLAS unit on its own). The asymmetry is not
  an early-season, zero-point, saturation, NEOWISE or difference-imaging artifact (referee 2, P = 0.95 that it is astrophysical).
- **Computed:** the data measure a source-lens *acceleration*. A constant-acceleration trajectory (same number of parameters; χ² 78.7) and
  xallarap with P ≥ 180 d (74-78) fit as well as annual parallax or better. Parallax is preferred on physical grounds: xallarap would need a
  ≥ 3 M☉ dark companion to a red-clump giant. Referee 2 puts P(annual parallax) = 0.80.
- **Computed (parallax model):** tE = 47.7 d with u0 = +0.14, or 51.8 d with u0 = −0.14 (ecliptic degeneracy, Δχ² = 1.9); 95% range 47-56 d.
  Our u0 sign convention is opposite to MulensModel's. t0 ≈ MJD 58012.2 (2017 Sep 16). A_max ≈ 7 (5.7-9.7, 95%).
- **Computed:** π_E,∥ = 0.62 (0.55-0.82 across systematics variants). π_E,⊥ = −0.86 is systematics-limited (−0.45 to −1.4 with a free 1%
  zero point for unit 02a). So **|πE| ≈ 0.6-1.6** (95%, errors inflated for correlated residuals). A jerk-parallax mirror solution at
  |πE| ≈ 1.6 is disfavoured by Δχ² ≈ 7-12 after inflation, not excluded. This is a *candidate* parallax measurement, not a measurement.
- **Computed:** fs = 1 (no blend) is assumed. With parallax and fs free, fs_o = 0.77 ± 0.24. The parallax model gives A = 2.73 at the
  NEOWISE visit (observed 2.731 in W1, 2.716 in W2).
- **Out-of-sample check:** the near-saturation nights left out of the fit reject the symmetric model, which predicts ×11 and ×16 against the
  observed ×5.98 and ×7.29. Parallax, acceleration and xallarap all predict them to within 10%. So the check is not evidence for
  parallax over the alternatives.
- **Achromaticity:** NEOWISE W1 = W2 to 0.5%. ATLAS c has only two nights (12 frames) in the event, both at A ≤ 1.10, so there is no ATLAS
  colour test. The per-band fs difference is 0.4σ with parallax.
- **The lens mass is not measured** (θE unknown). Along the mass-distance relation the lens is 0.1-0.65 M☉ at 0.5 kpc, or 0.04-0.26 M☉ at
  1 kpc: most likely an M dwarf within ~1 kpc. No brown-dwarf or mass claim belongs in a title, hook or brief.

## Predictions

1. **Gaia DR4** (scheduled 2026 Dec; epoch photometry and astrometry to 2020 Jan). For every asymmetric model: ΔG = −0.40 on 2017 Jul 27,
   −0.88 on Aug 22, and −2.07 on Sep 18 16:35 UT (the symmetric model gives −0.28 / −0.81 / −2.49). BP and RP must change by the same amount.
   The astrometric microlensing shifts at those transits are 0.07-0.32 θE, i.e. 0.3-0.5 mas for θE = 1-1.6 mas, which may be detectable at
   G = 13-14. That measures θE, and therefore the mass, and the direction separates parallax from xallarap.
2. Microlensing does not repeat: nothing comparable in any survey before 2017 or after. (A binary lens could repeat, but that is rare.)

## Next steps

- Gaia DR4, December 2026: the decisive dataset (above).

## Assessment

- Event is real and astrophysical: very high (two instruments).
- Microlensing: about 0.95 (referee 2: P(not microlensing) = 0.05).
- Asymmetry is astrophysical: about 0.95. It is annual parallax: about 0.8. The quoted |πE| interval is wide (0.6-1.6) on purpose.
- Novel: probably. Nothing in VSX, TNS (60"; within 15', only two unrelated 2023 objects), SIMBAD (CfA mirror, 30"), the Gaia alerts,
  Gaia DR3 or the web. No ATLAS17 designation. ADS unchecked.
- Importance: modest. It is a microlensing event toward the Galactic anticentre, where events are rare, on a bright giant, with a candidate
  parallax signal. It is not a new phenomenon.

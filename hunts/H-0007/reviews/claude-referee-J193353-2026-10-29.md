## Verdict

The 2020 brightening is real. NEOWISE shows it in 12 frames from 10 scans: the target is the only one of 2,021 stars within 20' that changed.
ZTF shows it independently, 70-100 days later and with the star's own colour: the target is the only one of 84 same-quadrant light curves (35 g, 49 r) that
moved. A point lens (tE ≈ 56 d, peak 2020 Apr 10 ± 7 d, A ≈ 1.95, χ² 229/423) fits both data sets. No flare, nova, dwarf nova,
supernova, blend or artifact fits them. **Probability that a mundane explanation is correct: 0.08.**

The residual risk has three parts:
- Only one day of NEOWISE samples the peak, and nothing optical exists before Jun 18.
- Gaia scanned the star three times during the predicted event without an alert.
- Several README statements are wrong or unsupported: the "slow decline", "peaking before Apr 9", the Apr 14 point, the stellar parameters and the ZTF range (see Required wording changes).

## Attacks and results

**1. Instrumental (b1, b2, b4, b6, b10).**
- *The event visit.* Apr 9-10 has 12 frames from 10 scans (15874r-15904r), MJD 58948.904-58949.885, plus one frame on Apr 14 (16034r, MJD 58954.136).
  - All 13 frames have cc_flags 0000 and sso_flg 0. ph_qual is AA except for the split detection (AU) and the Apr 14 frame (AB).
  - Three frames have qual_frame = 0. In one frame (15892r/113) the source is split (nb = 2, W2 14.9, W2 rχ² 60); the same scan's frame 115 is clean.
  - W1 = 12.317 against a 19-visit baseline of 13.056, so **ΔW1 = −0.739**. W2 = 12.397 against 13.136, so **ΔW2 = −0.739**.
    Both bands are ×1.98; the README's ×1.96 and −0.75/−0.74 agree within the visit scatter.
  - Visit-to-visit scatter elsewhere is 0.023 (W1) and 0.038 (W2). The colour change is −0.022, against a baseline colour scatter of 0.027-0.036.
  - Median rχ² in the event visit is 1.15 (W1) and 0.90 (W2), against baseline-visit medians of 0.80-1.52 and 0.52-0.88.
- *Controls in the same frames.* I took a 20' cone, W1 < 15, the event visit and the two adjacent visits (2019 Sep 29-Oct 1 and 2020 Sep 18-23): 88,849 detections.
  - 2,021 AllWISE sources are measured in all three visits. **The target is the only one with ΔW1 < −0.3.**
  - In the target's own range (W1 12.6-13.6, 390 stars), ΔW1 = +0.010 with MAD 0.025. At W1 9-11 it is +0.001 (MAD 0.007).
  - Frame by frame, the median of the 140-330 magnitude-matched controls in each frame is between −0.005 and +0.012. In those same frames the target is at −0.68 to −0.76.
- *Latents and ghosts.* I found no flag anywhere near the target:
  - All 80 event-visit detections within 60" of the target have cc_flags 0000.
  - The bright stars nearest the target in that visit are W1 5.2 at 12.7', W1 2.37 at 22.4', W1 3.06 at 22.9' and W1 1.20 at 54.8'.
  - A latent or ghost from any of them lands on a fixed detector offset, i.e. at a different sky position in each of 10 scans. It could not hold the source to 0.2" and keep W1−W2 constant.
  - Pass 3 found its latent cases because they carry P/H/D flags in 20-80% of frames and have rχ² 3-13× normal. This star has 0% flagged frames and an rχ² ratio of 1.1.
  - The one other pass-3 brightening near this epoch, J193806.08-151803.1 (6° away, 2020 Apr 11), is a flagged latent (79% of frames flagged, χ² ratio 3.1).
- *Moving objects.* The star lies **0.38° from the ecliptic**, so asteroids are common there. But it was observed at 88.4-89.4° solar elongation, where a main-belt asteroid moves about 0.25°/day. The centroid stayed fixed for 1 day, so this is excluded.
- *Moon and SAA.* During Apr 9-10 the Moon was 52-66° away and 89-95% illuminated, and no frame is moon-masked. Visit 8 (2018 Sep: 53°, 100%) and visit 7 (48°) had similar Moon conditions and are flat.
  - **The Apr 14 frame is moon_masked = 11, with the Moon 6.2° away.** Its controls sit at +0.022, so the frame is usable, but its bands disagree: W1 ×1.74 and W2 ×2.00 ± 0.30.
  - In the baseline visits, moon-masked frames scatter by 0.073 rms (as far as −0.13), while clean frames scatter by 0.042.
  - Four Apr 9-10 frames are at negative saa_sep. In the baseline visits, frames at negative saa_sep are unbiased (−0.001 ± 0.043).
- *The "slow decline within the visit".* I fitted the 8 clean frames (nb = 1, qual_frame > 0):
  - W1 slope is +0.083 ± 0.022 mag/d and W2 is +0.008 ± 0.057.
  - Fitted the same way, the 19 baseline visits have W1 slopes with rms 0.041 mag/d, reaching ±0.093. So this slope is **2σ and not a detection**.
  - **The decline is not established.**
- **Result: the NEOWISE brightening is real photometry of this source, not a frame-, scan- or calibration-level artifact.**

**2. Association and blends (b1, b5).**
- *Centroid.* The event-visit mean offset from the Gaia position is (−0.042 ± 0.025, −0.200 ± 0.024)". The 19 baseline visits average (−0.056 ± 0.040, −0.152 ± 0.040)".
  - The change is < 0.1" at 2σ. The extra light is 49% of the event flux, so its source lies **within ≈ 0.2" of the giant**.
  - The constant −0.15 to −0.20" Dec offset is present in every visit. It is the 2016→2020 proper motion (μδ = −11.7 mas/yr) plus a fixed frame offset, not a sign of a blend.
- *Gaia neighbours.* The 9.7" neighbour (G = 19.94, BP−RP 1.72, ipd_frac_multi_peak 1) would have to brighten by about 6 mag in W1. It would also move the centroid by ≈ 4.7", against an observed shift < 0.1". Excluded.
  - The next Gaia sources out to 30" are G = 18.2-20.5, at 15.8" and beyond.
  - Gaia DR3 for the target itself: RUWE 1.00, ipd_frac_multi_peak 0, ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude 0.045, non_single_star 0.
- *WISE neighbours.* No AllWISE source lies within 15". The nearest is W1 16.8 at 15.4" (W1−W2 = +0.57, the only red source within 30"). The nearest bright one is W1 10.98 at 31.7".
  The target's AllWISE W1−W2 is −0.03, and W3/W4 are upper limits. No red WISE source is blended with it.

**3. The ZTF tail (b3, b9).** The tail is real and it is achromatic.
- *Data.* All 2020 ZTF points come from one field, CCD and quadrant: 285 / 15 / 2. The second field (1327 / 6 / 2) has only 16 points, in 2021-2022.
  - The 2020 season starts on **Jun 18**. There is no ZTF point between 2019 Nov 5 and 2020 Jun 18, so ZTF never saw the peak.
- *Comparison stars.* I downloaded the IRSA DR light curves of everything within 3.6' at 14.3-16.8 mag: 82 comparison objects in the same quadrant, 12 of them matched in colour (g−r within 0.25 of the target's 0.77).
  - On each 2020 Jun-Jul exposure, their median offset is between −0.011 and +0.015.
  - After subtracting it, the target is at **−0.149 (g) / −0.168 (r) on Jun 18**, −0.131 (r) on Jun 24 and −0.122 (r) on Jul 2. On Jul 14-21 it is at −0.083 to −0.105 in both bands.
- *Uniqueness.* Over 2020 Jun 9-Jul 23, the target is the only one of 35 g-band and 49 r-band stars whose median offset is ≤ −0.08.
  The other stars have a median offset of 0.000 (g) and −0.004 (r), with MAD 0.007 and 0.005.
  In the same calendar window of 2018, 2019 and 2021-2025, the corrected offsets are −0.003 to +0.010.
- *Colour.* On Jul 14-21, Δg = −0.092 and Δr = −0.091 (corrected), so the extra light has g−r = 0.76 ± 0.15, the same as the star.
  A hot transient (g−r ≈ 0) would give Δg ≈ 2Δr, which is excluded at ≈ 5σ. On Jun 18 the extra light has g−r = 0.63 ± 0.15.
- *Before and after.* In 2020 Oct 6-28 the corrected offsets are −0.00 to −0.03 (median −0.02). In 2019 Oct 20-Nov 5 they are −0.01 to −0.026.
  Both are consistent with a lensing wing (the model gives −0.01 at both epochs).
- The README's range needs correcting. "0.10-0.18" understates the low end. With catflags = 0, the raw offsets are −0.08 to −0.18
  (g −0.082 on Jul 21). After the comparison-star correction they are −0.083 to −0.168.

**4. Physics: what else could add light of the star's own colour for about 3 months? (b5, b7; IRSA dust)**
- *The star.* Gaia DR3 gives G = 15.40, BP−RP 1.26, parallax 0.040 ± 0.041 and proper motion (−4.31, −11.70) mas/yr.
  - SFD/S&F E(B−V) is 0.087, so A_G ≈ 0.23 and the intrinsic BP−RP is ≈ 1.14. That makes the star **a K giant at Teff ≈ 4,900 K, not 5,840 K**.
    GSP-Phot's A_G = 0.84 is 3.6× the dust map, the usual extinction-temperature degeneracy.
  - The parallax puts it beyond about 6 kpc (2σ, including the zero point). There, 12.5 mas/yr means v_t ≳ 350 km/s, so this is a **halo giant**.
    FLAME's 2.8 M☉ and 0.46 Gyr are meaningless for it. MSC distance_msc = 939 pc is the usual MSC failure for a single giant.
  - ESP-ELS Hα pEW is +0.013 nm (no emission).
- *Flare (RS CVn or otherwise).* The extra W1 light equals the giant's own W1 light (about 50-75 L☉ in total) for ≥ 5 days. With the giant's own colour that is ≳ 10⁴¹ erg; if hot, 10⁴²⁻⁴³ erg.
  ZTF also shows ≥ 9% extra light 70-100 days later. No stellar flare does this. Excluded.
- *A dwarf nova or nova within 0.2" (foreground or background).*
  - Peak W1 ≈ 13.1 needs M_W1 ≈ +2-4 for a dwarf nova, i.e. one at 0.6-1.5 kpc that happens to lie within 0.2" of the giant.
  - The NEOWISE colour does **not** exclude it: a Rayleigh-Jeans source changes the colour by only +0.03, inside the −0.022 ± 0.035 observed. Free-free emission from a nova or symbiotic would change it by ≳ +0.2, which is excluded.
  - The tail excludes the dwarf nova. A disk in decline or a WZ Sge-type echo would add blue light (Δg ≈ 2Δr), and the extra light is the star's colour at ≈ 5σ.
  - A dwarf nova would also produce other outbursts. There are none in 20 NEOWISE visits or 7 years of ZTF (rms 0.010 with catflags = 0).
- *A supernova behind the star.* W1 ≈ 13 at peak needs ≲ 20 Mpc. The host would be a large galaxy, but there is no extended AllWISE source (ext_flg 0) and Gaia shows only stars within 30".
  The supernova would also have been V ≈ 12-13 and surely reported (see 6). Excluded.
- *Intrinsic variability of the giant.* NEOWISE is flat to 0.02 over 2014-2024 apart from this visit, ZTF r rms is 0.010 (catflags = 0), and the star is not variable in Gaia DR3.
  No pulsation or spot cycle gives a single achromatic ×2 in a K giant. Excluded.

**5. Microlensing plausibility (b7, b8, b10).**
- *Point-lens fit.* I fitted per-exposure NEOWISE W1/W2 (19 baseline visits plus the event) and ZTF g/r for 2018 Oct-2022 Jan, comparison-corrected and with a 0.012 mag floor.
  - **χ² = 229 for 423 points. t0 = MJD 58949.5 (2020 Apr 10), tE = 55.6 d, u0 = 0.575, A_max = 1.95. No blending.**
  - Profile ranges (Δχ² < 4): **t0 = Apr 2-17**, so the NEOWISE visit sits on the peak, not after it. **tE ≈ 48-62 d**; tE = 40 is at Δχ² = +54 and tE = 80 at +325.
  - Freeing the source fractions improves χ² by only 4.8 for 2 parameters (fs_W 0.12, fs_ZTF 0.60, u0 0.11). They are unconstrained.
  - Without the Apr 14 frame, t0 is Apr 12 and tE 54 d.
- *What the investigators claimed.* "Peaking before Apr 9 with tE ~ 50 d" is half right: the tE is right, but the peak is Apr 10 ± 7 d.
  - The best fit is flat across the visit. The Apr 14 frame (W1 ×1.74) is 2.6σ below it, while its W2 (×2.00) is not.
  - The ZTF tail does not prove the shape on its own. A ZTF-only fit is degenerate (t0 May 8, u0 1.2) and predicts only ×1.18 at the NEOWISE epoch.
  - The two data sets agree jointly, but the peak is sampled by one day of NEOWISE.
- *Checks against data the fit did not target.* The model predicts A = 1.017 at the 2020 Sep NEOWISE visit; observed W1 ×1.027, W2 ×1.019.
  It predicts −0.01 mag for ZTF in 2019 Nov and 2020 Oct; observed −0.02 ± 0.01. The ZTF Jun 18-Jul 21 pulls are within ±2.
- *Rate on this sightline.* With the same disc and bulge model as the first referee, plus a stellar halo: **τ ≈ 9×10⁻⁸** for D_S = 8.4 kpc (5.5×10⁻⁸ at 6 kpc).
  - That is 8× the J040025 sightline. With tE = 55 d, Γ ≈ 4×10⁻⁷ per star per year.
  - The chance that one snapshot of this star shows A ≥ 1.96 is τu² = 3×10⁻⁸, or 6×10⁻⁷ over its 20 visits.
  - Pass 3 tested 64.5 M faint stars over about 20 visits each. With a sky-averaged τ̄ of 1-3×10⁻⁸, about **4-13 such snapshots are expected**, before the pipeline's cuts.
  - So a microlensing event among the faint stars is **expected**. Finding one is not a coincidence that needs explaining. If anything, finding only one says pass 3 is incomplete.
- *Plausible lens.* With μ_rel ≈ 5-10 mas/yr, tE = 56 d means θE ≈ 0.8-1.5 mas. A 0.1-0.5 M☉ disc dwarf at 0.5-2 kpc fits.

## Most plausible mundane explanation remaining

None of the mundane explanations tested fits the data:
- An artifact is excluded by 2,021 controls in the same frames and by the independent ZTF tail.
- A blend is excluded by the < 0.1" centroid shift and the absence of neighbours within 9.7".
- A hot transient (flare, dwarf nova, nova) is excluded by the star-coloured ZTF tail and by energetics.
- A supernova is excluded by the absence of any host.

What remains is an unknown, slow (months-long), colour-neutral brightening of the halo giant itself that happens to mimic a point lens.
No such class is known for a quiet K giant. Most of the 0.08 comes from how thinly the light curve is sampled: one day at the peak, no
optical before Jun 18, and three Gaia transits that raised no alert. It does not come from any specific rival model.

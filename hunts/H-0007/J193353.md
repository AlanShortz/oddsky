## Evidence

- **Observed (NEOWISE, 2020 Apr 9-10):** 12 frames from 11 scans are ×1.98 brighter in both bands (ΔW1 = ΔW2 = −0.74) against a
  19-visit baseline (W1 13.06, robust scatter 0.02). No artifact flags, normal χ², centroid on the star. 2,021 stars within 20' in the
  same frames and the adjacent visits are unchanged (W1 12.6-13.6: +0.010, MAD 0.025); the target is the only one beyond −0.3 mag.
- **Observed (ATLAS, task 5271652):**
  - One event on 88 nights of 2020, seen by two units (01a, 02a) in both bands: ×2.42 on Mar 11 (the first night of the season),
    ×2.03 Apr 8, ×1.99 Apr 10 (NEOWISE ×1.98 on Apr 9-10), ×1.65 Apr 20-22, ×1.30 May 14-16, ×1.10 in July, ×1.05 in September, and
    within 2% by October.
  - The c-band excess is 1.05 ± 0.03 times the o-band excess.
  - No other good night in 2015-2026 is above ×1.10. Four nights above ×1.15 are all shallow frames (limit 14-17 mag); two of them also
    have a normal frame that night. One zero-flux dropout (2020 Jul 17, unit 01a) is excluded.
  - ATLAS has no 2020 data before Mar 11 or between Mar 11 and Apr 8, so **the peak and the rise are unobserved**.
- **Observed (ZTF, IRSA DR, catflags = 0, corrected with 12 colour-matched stars on field/CCD/quadrant 285/15/2):** the star is 0.17 mag
  bright on 2020 Jun 18 and 0.08-0.10 on Jul 14-21, achromatic (Δg = Δr to 0.01). It is the only one of 35 g and 49 r stars in its
  quadrant beyond −0.08. In the same Jun-Jul window of 2018, 2019 and 2021-2025 it sits at 0.00 ± 0.01. ZTF has no data between
  2019 Nov 5 and 2020 Jun 18.
- **Computed (second referee):** a point lens *without* parallax leaves the same asymmetry in all three surveys: the 2019 rising wing is
  2-3% below the model and the Jun-Sep 2020 tail 2-5% above. That is why such a fit under-predicts NEOWISE and ZTF. Annual parallax
  removes the asymmetry (joint χ² 715 → 460; ATLAS alone 419 → 244) with an ordinary lens of about 0.1-0.4 M☉ at 1-2 kpc.
- **Parallax point-lens fit to ATLAS, NEOWISE and ZTF jointly, 95% ranges:**
  - t0 = 2020 Mar 20-21 (Mar 17-22 across all models; Mar 11-25 from the data alone);
  - peak ×2.66-2.94 as observed (source magnified 2.6-4.1×);
  - tE = 40-55 d (median 45);
  - |u0| = 0.25-0.40;
  - π_E = 0.39-0.80;
  - source fraction 0.6-1.04 per survey, so no blend is needed.

  The investigators' first fit (no parallax: tE 63 d) and the first referee's (peak Apr 10, ×1.95) are superseded.
- **Computed (second referee):** on the decline alone, an exponential or a power-law decay fits far worse than the lens (χ² 413 and 728
  against 79). The decline speeds up after the first month and then slows: the lens shape. A transient that peaked by Mar 11 would
  only slow.
- **Computed (first referee):** the optical depth on this sightline is about 9×10⁻⁸. Among H-0007's 64.5 M faint stars, finding one such
  event on a NEOWISE visit is expected.

## Novelty (searched 2026-10-29)

Not found in VSX (120"), the ASAS-SN, ZTF, ATLAS or Gaia DR3 variable catalogues, TNS (60"), SIMBAD (CfA mirror, 120"), the Gaia Science
Alerts, KMTNet 2020 (nearest event 17° away), or a web search. The OGLE EWS index has no 2020 season, and MOA 2020 was not checked.

Gaia transited the star on 2020 Feb 28, Mar 12 and Apr 17. The parallax model predicts ΔG ≈ −0.6, −1.0 and −0.6 then. By Gaia's own
alert thresholds (Hodgkin et al. 2021) that should have produced a candidate; ATLAS measured ×2.42 the day before the Mar 12 transit. So
the missing alert says something about Gaia's alert system, not about the star.

## Alternatives

| explanation | status |
|---|---|
| Artifact in one survey | excluded: NEOWISE, ATLAS (two units, two bands) and ZTF see the same event; in NEOWISE and ZTF it is the only star in its field that changed |
| Blend | excluded: no source within 9.7"; centroid fixed; source fraction 0.6-1.04 |
| Flare, nova, dwarf nova, companion outburst | disfavoured: the extra light has the giant's colour from g to W2, and the decline's shape matches a lens, not an exponential or power-law decay |
| **Microlensing, with annual parallax** | fits all three surveys |

## Next steps and predictions

- ASAS-SN, once the service returns, can cover 2020 Feb-Mar: the two lens models differ by 0.17 mag on Feb 28, and the parallax model
  puts the peak at about −1.1 mag on Mar 20-21.
- Gaia DR5 (three transits during the event): predicted ΔG ≈ −0.6 (Feb 28), −1.0 (Mar 12), −0.6 (Apr 17), equal in BP and RP. DR4 reaches
  only the far wing (−0.03 to −0.05 in 2019 Nov).

## Assessment

- Real and astrophysical: very high (P(mundane) = 0.02, second referee).
- Microlensing: very likely, probably with annual parallax. The peak and the rise are unobserved.
- Novel: probably. Nothing in any catalogue or alert stream we reached.

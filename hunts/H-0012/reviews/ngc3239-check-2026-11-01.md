# NGC 3239 156.29 +17.16: independent check of the claimed obscured transient (2026-11-01)

The claim under test comes from `claude-referee-triage-2026-11-01.md` §2.1. Everything below was re-derived with
fresh code; the referee's scripts were only read. Scripts and outputs are in `ngc3239-check-2026-11-01/`. Raw
downloads (unWISE tile, HST/JWST/Spitzer images) are outside the repo, in `~/oddsky-runs/imaging/cache` and
`~/oddsky-runs/imaging/scratch/ngc3239-check/`. Nothing was committed or posted.

## Verdict

**Confirmed: a real, unreported, dust-obscured transient. The "dust-buried core-collapse SN" interpretation is
not confirmed.**

Three independent instruments see the same object:

| When | Instrument | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-11 to 2022 | WISE | A mid-IR outburst |
| 2021-02-16 | HST | An optical point source, which has vanished by 2022-12 |
| 2024-04 and 2024-05 | JWST (programme 3707, not used by the referee) | A new, unresolved warm-dust source 0.01" from the HST source, invisible at 1.5 µm |

Spitzer in 2008 shows nothing at that position.

The class of the transient is open:
- A uniform dust screen around a normal supernova does not fit. The HST colour is too blue for the extinction that
  the faintness would require.
- The IR output is 30-100× too low for a fully buried normal Type II SN at ≤ 200 days.
- An obscured luminous red nova (stellar merger), an ILRT-like event, or a low-energy or clumpy-dust supernova all
  remain possible.

Nobody has reported it: there is nothing in TNS, ATel, Gaia alerts, CBAT/Rochester, SPIRITS or the literature.

## 1. WISE: re-derived light curve, colour and position (`wise_lc.py`)

Method:
- unWISE neo11 epochal coadds of tile 1556p166, fetched fresh over 2 connections: 23 epochs, W1 and W2.
- References: per-season medians of the pre-2020.4 epochs.
- A 5×5 PSF-matching kernel fitted by least squares for each epoch.
- Forced photometry with an empirical PSF.
- At the bright epochs, a free PSF fit, tied to Gaia DR3 for each epoch (64 stars), with errors from
  injection-recovery (300 injections).

  - The rise is unresolved: under 205 days.

- **Colour.** W1−W2 = **0.95 ± 0.08** (Vega) at the peak and 0.93 ± 0.18 in April 2021. The triage had 0.84. That

## 2. HST (`hst_gaia_tie.py`, `hst_register.py`, `hst_diff.py`, `hst_census.py`)

**Coverage.** MAST has exactly three HST epochs covering the position, and none before the event. The first HST
image of this field is the 2021 one.

| date | programme | camera and filters |
|---|---|---|
| 2021-02-16 | 16179 (Filippenko SNAP, SN 2012A) | WFC3/UVIS F606W (2×355 s), F814W (2×390 s) |
| 2022-12-11 | 15446 (Dalcanton, Arp 263) | ACS/WFC F606W (2×390 s) |
| 2025-01-09 | 17502 (Thilker treasury) | WFC3/UVIS F275W, F336W, F438W, F555W, F814W, F657N |

**Registration.**
- The 2021 and 2022 single-visit drizzled images are aligned to Gaia eDR3. My check against Gaia DR3 gives offsets
  of −13/+0 and −3/+6 mas, with 8-12 mas scatter.
- The 2025 visit-level images are not aligned to Gaia (WCS from GSC 2.4.0; star scatter 50-200 mas). Their stars
  are broadened, and the third exposure in each filter is misregistered by about 90 mas. I used the two good
  single-exposure images per filter instead, registered them with an affine fit to 150-180 stars (8-12 mas rms),
  and combined them with cosmic-ray rejection.
- Every image was resampled onto the 2021 F606W grid (0.04").
- Differences use a least-squares 11×11 kernel and empirically rescaled noise.

**The source.**
- **Position:** 156.291224 +17.163778 (± 4 mas). The referee's position agrees to 0.035".
- **Offset from the WISE centroid: 0.37"** (dRA +0.15", dDec −0.34"), or 0.28" from the peak-epoch-only centroid.
  That is about 2σ with formal errors and about 1σ with the realistic WISE error. The triage's 0.87" was measured
  from the rounded coordinate.
- **Point-like.** Its Gaussian width is 1.04/0.94 px against 0.95/0.94 px for stars, and the PSF fit has
  χ²ν = 0.96.
- **Not a cosmic ray.** It is detected in each of the four 2021 exposures separately: S/N 11.8, 13.9 (F606W) and
  11.2, 13.2 (F814W) in exposure-minus-later-epoch differences.

**Photometry** (PSF fit, AB):

| image | flux (µJy) | magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| F606W 2021 − ACS F606W 2022 | 0.154 ± 0.008 (S/N 18; matched-filter 13.6) | **25.93** |
| F814W 2021 − F814W 2025 (same camera and filter) | 0.379 ± 0.019 (S/N 20; matched-filter 12.2) | **24.95** |
| ACS F606W 2022 direct | −0.017 ± 0.018 | > 27.1 (3σ): faded > 4× |
| F555W 2025 direct | 0.003 ± 0.054 (local scatter 0.027) | > 26.6 (3σ) |
| F814W 2025 direct | −0.13 ± 0.12 | consistent with 0 |
| F438W, F336W, F275W, F657N 2025 | consistent with 0 | limits 22.7-24.5 |

- The triage's F814W ≈ 25.3 came from a stack of 2021 and 2025, which dilutes it; 24.95 is the 2021-only value.
- **Colour:** F606W − F814W = 0.98 (AB) = 1.30 (Vega), so V−I ≈ 1.5.
- **Absolute magnitudes:** M_F606W = −3.55, M_F814W = −4.54 (AB, 7.9 Mpc).
- **Against the IR:** F606W − W1 = 7.4 mag (AB), with W1 interpolated to the HST epoch (140 µJy). The optical
  carries about 0.6% of the IR luminosity.
- **No host cluster or star** remains at the position in 2025: M_V > −2.9.

**Chance coincidence** (`hst_census.json`). Over 1,325 arcsec² around the source:
- Sources in the 2021−2022 F606W difference that are also positive in the 2021−2025 F814W difference
  ("variables", ≥ 8σ): 140, i.e. 0.106 per arcsec². P(one within 0.37") = 4.4%.
- Sources that, like this one, essentially vanish in 2022 and 2025 (≥ 8σ): 7, i.e. 0.0053 per arcsec².
  **P(within 0.37") = 0.23%**; P(within 0.6") = 0.6%.
- The nearest other "vanished" source is 5.9" away.
- Likelihood ratio, association against chance, with σ = 0.18": about 110 for the vanished class and about 6 even
  against all variables.
- The JWST coincidence (§3) makes the identification secure on its own.

**Could it be an ordinary variable?**
- **Cepheid: no.** At M_V ≈ −3.6 a Cepheid has an amplitude under 1 mag and never disappears. This source is absent
  to M_V > −2.4 in 2022 and > −2.9 in 2025.
- **Mira/LPV: no.**
  - V−I ≈ 1.5 is far too blue; Miras near maximum have V−I ≳ 2.5-5.
  - JWST F150W in 2024 shows nothing at the position (> 26.5 AB, M_H(AB) > −3.0). A Mira with M_I ≈ −5 would sit
    at M_K ≈ −8 to −9 at every phase.
  - The 3-µm source is new since 2008.
- **LBV/S Dor: no.** A quiescent LBV is M_V −7 to −10; nothing is there to −2.4.
- **Classical nova on its decline:** possible as a lone optical event. It cannot explain a 5.6×10⁵ L☉,
  1.5-year IR outburst, nor a new 3.6×10⁴ L☉ dust source lasting more than 3 years.
- **Variability across epochs and filters:** one detected epoch, 2021, seen in both filters and all four
  exposures. It is absent in all 2022 and 2025 filters.

## 3. JWST 2024: the decisive new evidence (`jwst_check.py`, `pointlike.py`)

Programme 3707 (PI Leroy, "A JWST Census of the Local Galaxy Population") imaged NGC 3239 with NIRCam on 2024-04-24
and 2024-05-22. The mosaics were registered to the HST F814W image with about 150 common stars (4-8 mas rms).

| filter | date | flux at the HST position (µJy) | AB | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F150W | 2024-04-24 | −0.008 ± 0.030 | > 26.5 | nothing |
| F150W | 2024-05-22 | −0.006 ± 0.038 | > 26.3 | nothing |
| F300M | 2024-04-24 | **3.17 ± 0.06** | 22.65 | free fit 8 mas from the HST position |
| F300M | 2024-05-22 | **3.65 ± 0.08** | 22.50 | 10 mas |
| F335M | 2024-04-24 | **5.90 ± 0.04** | 21.97 | 9 mas |
| MIRI F770W, F2100W | 2024-05-22 | — | — | not covered (position 1.7" off the valid area) |

- The source is unresolved, with Gaussian widths 0.95/0.96/0.87 px against 0.88-0.92 px for stars.
- The F300M + F335M blackbody gives **T ≈ 580 K, L ≈ 3.6×10⁴ L☉ (7.9 Mpc), R ≈ 90 AU**. F335M holds the 3.3 µm
  PAH band, so T may be lower.
- This predicts W1 ≈ 6 and W2 ≈ 18 µJy in 2024. That matches the residual WISE level of about +15 µJy.
- The 15% rise in F300M between the two epochs is at the level of calibration and should not be over-read.

## 4. Spitzer 2008: no progenitor (`spitzer_check.py`)

The only IRAC epoch is 2008-01-02 (LVL); there are no warm-mission visits. The SEIP catalogue has no source within
3".

| band | measured (µJy) | expected if the 2024 source had been there (µJy) |
|---|---|---|
| 3.6 µm | 0.9 ± 0.9 | 7.5 (8σ) |
| 4.5 µm | 1.2 ± 1.1 | 16 (15σ) |

- Errors are the confusion scatter of fits at random positions within 15".
- So the 2024 dust source is new. Combined with the flat WISE light curve for 2010-2020, it appeared with the
  2020 event.
- **The limit on a dusty progenitor** is νLν(4.5 µm) < 4×10³ L☉ (3σ, 7.9 Mpc). That is below the progenitor of
  SN 2008S (L ≈ 3.5×10⁴ L☉ at ~440 K, νLν(4.5) ≈ 10⁴ L☉), which argues against a SN 2008S-type ILRT
  progenitor.
- 8.0 µm has 15 ± 6 µJy, which is local PAH emission.
- PS1 2010-2014 has nothing within 2". Its nearest source is 2.34" SE, constant at g ≈ 21.1, a knot. That knot is
  what ZTF's "quiescent r ≈ 20.3" measured.

## 6. Physics

**Not an AGN.** NGC 3239 is IB(s)m pec (Arp 263) with no nucleus. The source is 70.6" east of the optical centre
(2.7 kpc at 7.9 Mpc, about 0.47 a26). The JWST source is also new and dark at 1.5 µm.

**Luminosity and timescale.**
- The IR at first detection is M_W2 = −15.1 to −15.7 (Vega), 5.6-9×10⁵ L☉ at 960 K, decaying with a 169-day
  e-fold.
- That sits above the SPIRITS "SPRITEs" (M_[4.5] −11 to −14, Kasliwal+2017) and at or below the faint end of the
  SPIRITS obscured SNe (Jencson+2019, roughly M_[4.5] −16 to −18; quoted from memory, to be checked against the
  paper).
- The peak may have been missed by up to about 6 months.

**Problems for a "dust-buried normal CCSN" (A_V ≳ 6):**
1. **Colour.** For a SN at 85-290 days, V−I ≈ 1.5 allows only E(V−I) ≈ 0.4 (A_V ≈ 0.6-2.2, R_V = 3.1). But the
   faintness would need A_V ≈ 9-20 (M_I ≈ −4.5 against −10.5 to −17). A uniform screen cannot do both. The optical
   would have to be leaked or scattered light (clumpy dust, an echo) or seen through grey (large-grain) dust.
2. **Energy.** A fully enshrouded SN IIP with ≥ 0.03 M☉ of ⁵⁶Ni would re-radiate about 3×10⁷ L☉ at ≤ 205 days
   (the latest possible phase at the first WISE detection). The observed 5.6-9×10⁵ L☉ is 30-100× below that.
   Only a very low-⁵⁶Ni event (≲ 0.003 M☉), or dust covering only our sightline, fits.

**Alternatives that fit more naturally:**
- **An obscured luminous red nova (stellar merger).**
  - E_IR ≳ 3-5×10⁴⁶ erg and a peak of about 10⁶ L☉ are within the LRN range.
  - LRN remnants become cool, dusty, IR-luminous sources (here 580 K, 3.6×10⁴ L☉ after 3.4 yr).
  - However, late LRNe are usually redder than V−I ≈ 1.5.
- **An ILRT-like eruption.** The energy is fine, but there is no IR-bright progenitor at the SN 2008S level.
- **A low-energy CCSN behind clumpy CSM dust.** Not excluded.

The 2024 dust source needs ongoing power: an IR echo, CSM interaction, or a surviving or merged star.

**Discriminating next steps:**
- JWST/NIRSpec + MIRI spectroscopy of the remnant (it is bright: F335M = 22.0 AB). Molecular bands (CO, H₂O)
  would point to a merger; [Ni II]/[Fe II] or silicate dust to a SN.
- A deeper radio limit.
- The NEOWISE single-exposure light curve inside the 2020-11 visit.
- SPHEREx (2025+).

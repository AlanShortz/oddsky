**The J182124 control reproduces the OGLE event only weakly.** The reasons:
- A G 15.0 star 5.2″ away carries most of the ATLAS PSF flux. The target's share is 0.14-0.27 in o and 0.10-0.20 in c.
- ATLAS's 2015 coverage starts on Jul 25, a week before OGLE's ×1.76 peak.

What ATLAS does see:
- c over Aug 9-Oct 22 reads +32 ± 12 uJy, +0.30 ± 0.11 of the Gaia star's c flux (2.7σ).
- A lens on the star predicts about +0.35 there, before the ATLAS flux-scale factor (§2.3).
- The joint fit gives the same ATLAS scale (s = 0.44, c = o) as the other events.
- o is uninformative (per-night scatter 150-300 uJy).

So the control does not contradict the method, but it shows that a crowded-field non-detection by ATLAS means little. Every
statement below therefore carries the predicted S/N.

## 2. Method and what it found about ATLAS

### 2.1 Reduction (a2, a4)

**Cuts.**
- c/o only; err == 0, dujy > 0, mag5sig > 17.5. No chi/N cut.
- Nightly weighted means per (band, unit, local night), with a 4σ clip.

**Baselines are not detected for six of the eight.** Medians of nights more than 400 d from the event:
- **Negative** for J180313, J182811 and J174029 (o −22 to −146 uJy, c −18 to −64), and for J184837 and J174036 in c. This is
  the crowded-field sky bias seen in H-0008.
- Median/scatter < 3 for J184837, J174036 and J184922 in o.
- Only J200958 (o S/N 20-25, c 12-16) and J182124 (S/N 3-4) are detected.

So for six candidates **no ATLAS flux ratio is formed**. The test is in excess flux, which an additive offset does not bias.

**PSF era.**
- Every event night is in the wide-PSF era: 02a in 2015-16 (maj 3.5-6.2 px); 01a in 2017 (3.0-4.0 px).
- In crowded fields the forced flux runs with PSF width by up to 260 uJy/px (`a2_reduce.log`). A linear PSF correction
  extrapolated from narrow-PSF years fails badly at maj > 4.5 px: J182124's o event nights come out −500 to −780 uJy.
- Each night and frame is therefore compared with its K nearest quiet nights or frames in PSF-shape space: maj, min,
  e·cos 2φ, e·sin 2φ. The orientation terms matter because the dominant neighbours sit at fixed position angles.
- Comparison frames come from the same unit and band, more than 150 d from the event.
- The Gaia light curves are flat there: G scatter 0.8-3% for the bright four, 9-10% for J184922 and J182811.

**Strictly > 400 d with a matched PSF is possible only for J184837** (01a 2017-19). For the 2015-16 events the matching
wide-PSF nights lie 150-400 d from the event. Gaia shows those nights quiet.

**Variants that do not change the answers.**
- Nightly kNN with an error model calibrated on quiet nights (a4). Quiet-night robust z-scatter is 0.57-1.09; errors were never
  deflated.
- Frame-level kNN (a5).
- Same-detector-position quiet frames (a6).
- Comparison frames restricted to the same PSF era: 02a before 2017, 01a before 2020 (a7).

### 2.2 Blending (a1)

Every Gaia source within 10″ was weighted by the overlap of Gaussian PSFs, exp(−r²/4σ²), at FWHM 3.8 px (2015-17) and 2.6 px.

### 2.3 The ATLAS flux scale

Wherever the event is detected, ATLAS's excess is 0.45-0.7 of what the Gaia star's converted ATLAS-band flux predicts. Joint
fits give s_o:

| J182124 | J182811 | J200958 | J184922 | J184837 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.44 | 0.34-0.46 | 0.53 | 0.57 | 0.70 |

The two detected baselines show the same factor:
- J200958: the ATLAS o baseline is 0.72 of the Gaia-predicted PSF-weighted flux (c 0.63).
- J182124: 0.87 in o.

So this is mostly the flux scale of ATLAS reduced-image photometry against my Gaia→c/o conversion for these very red stars
(BP−RP 2.4-3.4). It is not a sign that the events are smaller in ATLAS. A ratio test that uses Gaia shares (J200958) is immune to
it. **An achromatic event on the Gaia star gives α ≈ 0.45-0.7 in either band; α ≈ 0 in a band means that band does not follow.**

### 2.4 Repeats (a4)

**Window.** 20-d bins in each band, pooled over units, 2017.2-2026.8. J184837 is covered from 2015.6. The 2015-16 (02a) and
2017-19 (01a) wide-PSF seasons are excluded away from the events, because their matched comparison nights lie within 150 d. Gaia
DR3 covers 2014.7-2017.4.

**Result.** No candidate has any bin above 4σ in either band, nor any bin below −5σ.

**Sensitivity.** The largest bins correspond, in raw units, to:

| J200958 | J184837 | J182124, J182811, J174029 | J174036 | J180313 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ×1.05 | ×1.15 | ×1.5 | ×2.4 | ×2.0 |

After the flux-scale factor, a repeat of each star's own Gaia amplitude would have been seen at > 4σ in one bin for all except
J174036 (marginal) and J182124 (o noise).

**Conclusion.** All eight are single in Gaia 2014.7-2017.4 plus ATLAS 2017-2026.

## 3. Per candidate

### 3.1 J182124.77-190909.7 (positive control) (a3-a8)

**Event coverage.**
- OGLE BLG783.17.33659 rose from ×1.13 (Jun 1) to ×1.76 (Aug 1), was ×1.54 on Aug 25, and returned to ×1.15 on Oct 29.
- ATLAS observed:
  - o on Jul 25 (4 frames);
  - c on Aug 8-11 (14 frames);
  - o on Sep 23-Oct 26;
  - c on Oct 9-22.

**Event nights (same era, PSF-matched).**
- o Jul 25: +119 ± 145 uJy, where a lens on the star predicts ≈ +180 before scaling.
- c Aug 9-11: +57 ± 25, −10 ± 23, +27 ± 24 (prediction +63).
- c Oct 14-22: +70 to +158 ± 40-65 (prediction +14-19).
- Pooled c: **+32 ± 12 uJy (2.7σ)**. Pooled o: +48 ± 96.

**Ratios.** o ×1.02 ± 0.05 against ×1.05 predicted at the ±30 d nights (a3). c ×1.03 ± 0.01.

**Joint Gaia + ATLAS fit.**
- Lens (fs = 1, t0 Aug 12, tE 54 d, u0 0.76) with s = 0.44.
- c and o scales are equal (0.44 and 0.46; Δχ² 0.0).
- ATLAS χ² is 17.8 for 11 nights under every shape.

**Frames.** Quiet-frame z tails in o and c are 2% above 3σ. This field is the worst of the eight.

**Verdict.**
- ATLAS sees the event at the expected epoch and scale, but at 2.7σ.
- It confirms nothing new about this already-published event.
- Its main use is to calibrate how little ATLAS can say in a field like this.

### 3.2 J200958.93+321427.9 (a3-a8)

**ATLAS o on Dec 6-9, 2015.**
- +123 ± 30, +117 ± 41, +95 ± 26 and +110 ± 25 uJy: **+109 ± 14 uJy, ratio ×1.29 ± 0.04** over the matched baseline of
  ≈ 373 uJy.
- Every one of the 14 frames is high, at 5 detector positions; each frame's empirical tail probability is ≤ 0.03.
- Gaia G is ×1.41 on Nov 12 and ×1.44 on Dec 15. A brightening on the Gaia star, which holds 0.73 of the ATLAS o flux at this
  PSF, predicts ×1.31.
- **Time, amplitude and blend share all agree.** NEOWISE (referee) showed the centroid moving onto the star. ATLAS's amplitude
  says the same.

**Before and after.**
- Oct 5-Nov 1 ATLAS reads +2 to +46 ± 22-66 uJy, about +0.07 of the star.
- The Gaia lens interpolation gives about +0.15-0.31 there.
- α_pre is 0.24 ± 0.15, against 0.63 ± 0.08 at the peak: a 2σ hint that the rise is steeper than Gaia's lens template.
- In the joint fit ATLAS χ² is 8.5 for 16 nights (lens) and 9.5 (asymmetric PV7). Lens and bump are equal (BIC 66.4 against 67.1).
- After Dec 9 the field goes into solar conjunction. Mar 16-20 is flat (−7 ± 31, +9 ± 58).

**Colour.** c exists only on Oct 19-21 (−40 d): +11 ± 10 uJy against +14 predicted. There is no colour test at the peak.

**Repeats.** None 2017.3-2026.8. The largest bin is +2.0σ, ×1.05 of the star.

**Verdict.**
- ATLAS confirms the event independently in o, at the amplitude a brightening of the Gaia star requires. Ten years without a
  repeat count mildly against a Be-star outburst; Be outbursts often recur.
- What does not change: ATLAS cannot separate a lens from a bump, and it gives no colour at the peak. An Hα spectrum remains the
  decisive test.
- P 0.40 → 0.45.

### 3.3 J184837.12-051620.2 (a3-a8)

**ATLAS at the peak.**
- o on 2017 Mar 14-15, two days before Gaia's ×1.56 transit.
- 01a, two frames at two detector positions: +168 and +268 uJy (z 2.2, 2.7; same era +200 ± 60).
- 02a, single frames: +40 ± 87 and +92 ± 100.
- ±30 d pooled: **+111 ± 48 uJy = +0.39 ± 0.17 of the star**. A lens on the star predicts +0.53 (α 0.76 ± 0.32).

**After the peak.** May 8-13 (+48 to +53 d; Gaia ×1.20 on May 7), same era, reads +16 ± 20 uJy = +0.06 ± 0.07 of the star,
against +0.17-0.20 predicted (+0.08-0.14 after the flux scale).

**Fits and colour.** Joint fit: s_o = 0.70; lens BIC 49.6 against 52.1 for an asymmetric Gaussian. No c within 60 d.

**Repeats.** None, 2015.6-2026.8.

**Verdict.** Consistent but weak (2.3σ at the peak). It neither adds nor removes the referee's 2σ "bluer when brighter". P 0.30.

### 3.4 J174036.98-223251.4 (a3, a4, a5)

**Coverage.**
- Gaia's event ran from 2017 Jan 30 (×1.87) through Feb 6 (×3.1) to Mar 16 (×1.11).
- ATLAS's first nights of the season are Mar 14-15 (+32 d), where a lens predicts +0.11 of the star.
- ATLAS reads +0.20 ± 0.53. Predicted S/N is 0.1.

**Why so weak.** The target is 8-14% of the ATLAS flux: a G 16.3 star at 4.0″ and 34 Gaia sources within 10″. Per-frame

**Verdict.** No coverage. P 0.30.

### 3.5 J180313.03-150702.0 (killed by the referee) (a3-a8)

**PSF.** Most event nights (Oct 13-26) have maj 5.0-6.2 px. That is beyond any quiet comparison frame (kNN distance 1.3-2.5 px),
and their residuals are strongly negative (−100 to −356 uJy, PSF systematics). Only the frames with kNN distance < 1 px are used.

**Event nights.**
- o Sep 23-24: +61 ± 65 and +77 ± 131.
- o Oct 2: +277 (one frame).
- c Oct 9-22: −8 ± 17 pooled over 5 nights.
- Against a lens on the star: α_o 0.8 ± 0.8, α_c −0.3 ± 1.0.

**Colour.** The referee's Gaia result was "bluer when brighter" (k 2.2 ± 0.4). If true, c should rise more than o. ATLAS's c
does not, but at S/N ≈ 1.

**Verdict.** Consistent but weak. ATLAS can neither confirm nor refute the chromaticity. P 0.10.

### 3.6 J184922.92-000203.9 (Hα emitter) (a3-a8)

**A real rise, before Gaia's peak.**
- ATLAS o on 2016 Oct 9, 11, 15 and 19: +74 ± 20, +176 ± 23, +152 ± 20 and +130 ± 25 uJy. 23 frames at 6 detector positions;
  most frames are beyond every quiet frame (p_emp ≤ 0.0012).
- Pooled: **+3.42 ± 0.28 of the star's o flux**, about ×7 of the star after the flux scale.
- Gaia had ×1.54 on Sep 20 and ×7.43 on Nov 11 (RP ×8.5), so the source was already near its peak brightness a month before
  Gaia's brightest transit.

**c is unusable.**
- Oct 1 and Oct 31 read −55 ± 18 and −100 ± 17 uJy. All frames are low, at z −2 to −6 against quiet frames at the same detector
  position.
- That is 5-10× the star's whole c flux (11 uJy), so it is not astrophysical. An unexplained field artefact.
- Oct 27 reads +32 ± 15.

**Shape.**
- Gaia + ATLAS o: an asymmetric Gaussian fits with a 12-d rise and an 80-d decline (peak about Oct 17), s_o = 0.57, χ² 33.7.
- The static point lens (fs ≤ 1) gives χ² 136: **ΔBIC −99**.
- I did not refit the referee's parallax solution (tE ≈ 890 d, u0 ≈ 0, fs_G 0.11).
- A rise about six times faster than the decline is hard for any point lens.

**Repeats.** None, 2017.5-2026.8.

**Verdict.**
- ATLAS confirms the event and contradicts a static-lens shape.
- Together with the Hα excess, an intrinsic outburst (YSO, Be star or symbiotic-like) is the reading.
- No repeat in ten years argues only against a frequently recurring dwarf nova.
- P 0.15 → 0.10.

### 3.7 J182811.16-082538.6 (a3-a8)

**o before the peak.**
- Single frames on Oct 2 and Oct 3, 2015, 2-3 d before Gaia's ×9.1 transit (Oct 5): +382 ± 68 and +266 ± 84 uJy.
- Neither is matched by any of the 834 quiet o frames (p_emp < 0.0012 for both).
- Quiet frames at the same detector position read +9 ± 42, which gives local z 7.0 and 4.6.
- Sep 23 (−16 d; Gaia ×2.0 on Sep 8) has 4 frames: +82 ± 38.
- Oct 23 (+14 d) has 2 frames: +172 and +132 (z 2.1, 1.5).
- ±30 d pooled: **+2.32 ± 0.43 of the star**.
- The event is real at the Gaia star's position and is not a Gaia scan artefact. That answers the referee's worry about baseline
  scatter and scan-angle bins.

**c after the peak.**
- Oct 9-16 (0 to +7 d), 14 frames at 3 positions: **+0.3 ± 9.0 uJy**.
- An achromatic lens on the star predicts +21-55 uJy before scaling: +10-38 after it.
- Joint fits: s_c = −0.17 against s_o = 0.46 for the unblended lens (Δχ² 4.1, ~2σ). For the blended lens that fits best,
  s_c −0.19 against s_o 0.34 (Δχ² 1.8).
- Gaia's two peak transits give BP ×2.8 and ×12.8 (inconsistent: faint BP at 21.9), against RP ×8.3 and G ×9.1.
- Two independent hints that the blue band does not follow.

**Shape.** The unblended lens is worse than a blended one by Δχ² 9.6 (fs 0.075, tE 343 d, u0 0.003). The blended lens equals an
asymmetric PV7 (ΔBIC +2.8).

**Verdict.** Consistent but weak: real and single, but chromatic at 1.3-2σ in ATLAS. P 0.30 → 0.25.

**Coverage.**

## 4. What the hunt should take from this

1. **ATLAS cannot test most Gaia DR3-era (2014-17) faint plane events.** Four of the eight have usable ATLAS data within 30 d
   (J200958, J184837, J182811, J182124), and only J200958 at high S/N. The 2015-16 Hawaii-only, wide-PSF era is the limit, along
   with G ≈ 19-20 targets on negative crowded-field baselines.
2. **Use the predicted S/N, not the detection.** The control's S/N is about 2.4; J174036's and J174029's are 0.1. "Not seen by
   ATLAS" is evidence only where the predicted S/N exceeds about 5. That holds for J200958 (13), J182811 (17 in o, 3-5 in c) and
   J184922 (10).
3. **The ATLAS/Gaia flux scale is about 0.5-0.7 for these red stars.** Compare in excess flux with a free scale, or as ratios
   with Gaia-derived blend shares. Do not compare against the raw Gaia-converted flux.
4. **Linear PSF corrections fail at maj > 4.5 px.** Match frames in PSF-shape space (maj, min, orientation) within the same era.
   Frames with no quiet match within 1 px cannot be used (J180313's Oct 13-26).

## 5. Unchecked

- **ATLAS difference-image photometry.** It would remove the crowded-field sky bias and the J184922 c artefact. Only the
  reduced-image files were requested. A difference-image request for J182811, J184922 and J200958 is the obvious next step.
- **Image cutouts** of J182811 Oct 2-3 (single frames) and J184922 c Oct 1/31. Not available through the forced-photometry
  service.
- **ASAS-SN Sky Patrol** (J200958 at V ≈ 19-20 is beyond it), OGLE-IV photometry for J182811 and J180313, and VVV Ks for the bulge
  ones.
- **The 5 inconclusive events and the 3 cases** listed in the README as ATLAS-testable were not queued.
